  1. Use a pair of results you have, or these: prompt A passed 42 of 50 examples, prompt B
     passed 44 of 50 on different examples.
  2. For each, compute the standard error √(p × (1 − p) ÷ n) and the 95% band p ± 2 SE.
  3. Compute the standard error of the difference: √(SE_A² + SE_B²). Is the gap bigger than

  ```python

  def se(passed, n):
      p = passed / n
      return p, sqrt(p * (1 - p) / n)
  (pa, sa), (pb, sb) = se(42, 50), se(44, 50)
  print(pa, sa, pb, sb, "gap", pb - pa, "2*SE_diff", 2 * sqrt(sa**2 + sb**2))
  ```

**You need:** square roots. → mean-variance-standard-deviation first.

Two numbers look alike and answer different questions. The **standard deviation** says how much
individual values vary. The **standard error** says how much your *average* would change if you
ran the whole measurement again. An eval pass rate is an average, so the uncertainty around it is
a standard error, and mixing the two up leads to both false alarms and false confidence.

## The thought experiment

Grade 50 random outputs and compute the pass rate. Now imagine doing it again with 50 fresh
outputs, and again, and again. Each pass rate differs a little. Plot them and you get a narrow
distribution of means, and its standard deviation is the **standard error**.

You don't need to repeat anything to get it. For a mean of measurements:

- **SE = s ÷ √n**, where s is the sample standard deviation.

For a pass rate (a mean of 0s and 1s), s works out to √(p × (1 − p)), so:

- **SE = √(p × (1 − p) ÷ n)**.

A rough 95% band is the estimate ± 2 SE. The worst case is p = 0.5, where 2 SE is exactly
**1/√n**, which gives a handy rule of thumb:

| n | rough 95% band |
|---|---|
| 20 | ±22 points |
| 100 | ±10 points |
| 400 | ±5 points |
| 2,500 | ±2 points |

Small evals cannot detect small improvements. To halve the band you need four times the data.

## Worked example: is 88% better than 84%?

A prompt tweak moves the pass rate from 84% to 88% on 50 test questions.

1. **SE of each rate** (using p ≈ 0.86): √(0.86 × 0.14 ÷ 50) ≈ 0.049, about **5 points**.
2. **SE of the difference.** Variances add when you subtract two independent estimates:
   √(0.049² + 0.049²) ≈ 0.069, about **7 points**.
3. **Compare:** the gap is 4 points, well inside 2 × 7 = 14. On this evidence the two prompts
   are indistinguishable.

The fix is cheaper than it looks: run both prompts on the **same** 50 questions and look only at
the questions where they disagree. Shared difficulty cancels out, as
→ hypothesis-testing-null shows.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the SD as the uncertainty of the mean.** It overstates uncertainty by a factor of √n.
- **Using the SE to describe single answers.** It understates how much one response can vary.
- **Comparing two rates by their own bands only.** The difference has its own, wider standard
  error.
- **Expecting double the data to halve the noise.** It takes four times.

## Where you'll see it in AI

Error bars on eval scores and leaderboards (→ evaluation-metrics), deciding how big an eval set
must be, and reading weekly quality dashboards. The ±2 SE band works because averages follow a
bell curve, which → central-limit-theorem explains next.
