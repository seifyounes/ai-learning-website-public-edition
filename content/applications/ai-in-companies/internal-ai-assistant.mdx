Every company runs on knowledge that lives in too many places: Drive, Notion, email threads,
someone's head. An **internal AI assistant** turns that scattered knowledge into something every
employee can simply *ask*. "What's our refund policy?" "How do we onboard a new client?" "Where's
the latest pricing deck?" Answered in seconds, grounded in your real documents. This is the
second-brain idea scaled from one person to a whole organization.

## How it works: RAG over your docs

The engine is **Retrieval-Augmented Generation** (→ rag-fundamentals). Instead of hoping the
model "knows" your company, you:

1. **Ingest** your documents and split them into chunks.
2. **Embed** the chunks into a vector store so they're searchable by meaning.
3. At question time, **retrieve** the most relevant chunks and hand them to the model along with
   the question.
4. The model **answers from those chunks and cites them.**

That citation step is what makes it trustworthy: staff can click through and verify, instead of
wondering whether the bot made it up. If you don't code, you don't have to build this pipeline
yourself. Tools like Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM), a ChatGPT or Claude Project, or a
Gemini Gem with uploaded files do a version of it for you.

## Privacy is a first-class decision

Internal docs are sensitive: contracts, salaries, customer data. *Where the model runs* matters
as much as how well it answers:

- **No-code platforms**: fastest to stand up; check their data-handling and no-training terms.
- **RAG pipeline you control**: more work, full control over where data goes.
- **Local / self-hosted models**: nothing leaves your machines; the right call for the most
  sensitive documents.

Access matters as much as location. An assistant must not let someone read, through a chat
window, a document they couldn't open directly. Either keep restricted documents out of a shared
index, or make retrieval respect each user's permissions. The governance lesson goes deeper
(→ governance-security); at minimum, don't paste confidential docs into a consumer chatbot to save
an afternoon.

## Garbage in, confident garbage out

The hardest truth about knowledge-base bots: **most wrong answers are document problems, not
model problems.** If two SOPs contradict each other, the bot may confidently pick one. If the
policy was never written down, it can't retrieve it. Building the assistant doubles as an audit
of your documentation, and that cleanup is half the value. Give every source a named owner and a
review date, or the assistant will slowly drift out of date.

## Worked example: an HR policy assistant

A 40-person company wants staff to stop emailing HR the same ten questions.

1. **Pick the sources.** The current handbook, the leave policy, the expenses policy, the
   remote-work policy, and onboarding guides: eight documents. Salary bands stay out.
2. **Clean before indexing.** You find two handbooks (2023 and current). The old one is
   archived, not indexed. Each document gets an owner.
3. **Chunk by heading.** Policies split on their section headings, so a citation points to
   "Leave policy, 3.2 Carry-over" rather than a random paragraph.
4. **Ask real questions.** Take ten questions HR actually received last month.
5. **Grade every answer.** Supported by the cited chunk? Complete? Any answer from general
   knowledge? Two answers fail: one because the expenses policy never mentions mileage (a
   document gap), one because the question said "time off" and the policy says "leave" (a
   retrieval gap, fixed by adding the common phrasing to the document).
6. **Launch to one team**, with a feedback button, before the whole company.

> 💡 **Try it:** Upload two documents to Gemini Notebook, a ChatGPT or Claude Project, or a
> Gemini Gem, then ask a question the documents *don't* answer. Does it say so, or does it guess? Five
> minutes tells you how much the grounding instruction matters.

## Common failure modes

- **Confident, wrong answers** → stale or duplicate documents in the index → archive old
  versions and give each source an owner.
- **"Not in the docs" when it is** → the question's wording doesn't match the document, or chunks
  split the answer → chunk by heading and add the words people actually use.
- **It reveals restricted information** → sensitive documents indexed for everyone → remove them
  or enforce per-user permissions at retrieval.
- **Nobody uses it** → launched with an email and no training → treat it as a rollout (next
  lesson).

## From answering to acting

The next level is connecting the assistant to tools via **MCP** (→ mcp-deep) so it can *do*
things, not just talk: look up an order status, file a ticket, draft a reply in context. Start
with read-only access and add actions one at a time.

## Next

You've built real capabilities. None of it matters if people don't actually use it; rolling it
out to humans is the hard part.
