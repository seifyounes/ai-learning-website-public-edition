You built it, you priced it. Now you have to make sure people actually hear about it. The
hard truth: **"if you build it, they will come" is a myth.** A launch is a campaign you run on
purpose, and even a small one beats releasing in silence. None of it requires code; it's
writing, recording and showing up.

## Launch is a campaign, not a moment

Think in three phases.

- **Before (weeks).** Build an audience: share progress publicly, collect a waitlist (your
  validation work doubles as launch fuel). Prepare the assets. Line up the people who said
  they'd care, so launch day doesn't start from zero.
- **During (a day or a week).** Post in your chosen channels, reply to every comment quickly,
  and stay available. Early replies shape how a thread or listing goes.
- **After (weeks).** Email everyone who signed up, watch what new users actually do, fix the
  biggest stumbling block, and write down what you learned for the next launch.

## The four assets

1. **A positioning line.** One sentence on who it's for, the problem, and what's different. A
   useful template: "For [who] who [problem], [product] [does what]. Unlike [the usual
   alternative], it [key difference]."
2. **A short demo.** 30–60 seconds showing the single moment where the core value appears. No
   intro, no logo animation. Screen recording plus a voice-over is enough.
3. **Screenshots** of the core flow, captioned with outcomes, not feature names.
4. **One call to action.** "Join the waitlist", "Try it free" or "Book a call". One. More
   choices mean fewer clicks.

AI is useful for drafting and tightening all four: ask a chat assistant (ChatGPT, Claude,
Gemini) for ten positioning lines and pick the one a stranger understands fastest, or have it
cut your demo script to 60 seconds.

## Pick your channels

Go where your users already are:

- **Product Hunt**: a concentrated one-day spike, strongest with tech-savvy early adopters.
- **Hacker News (Show HN)**: for technical products; plain, honest posts work best there.
- **Communities**: subreddits, Discord servers, Slack groups and forums where the problem
  lives. Read each one's self-promotion rules first.
- **Your own audience**: email list, LinkedIn, X, a newsletter.
- **Offline or direct**: for local or B2B products, messaging 20 likely customers can beat any
  launch site.

Don't spread thin. Pick the two or three that fit and go hard.

## Worked example: a shopping-list app's first launch

The app turns a recipe link into a clean shopping list. The builder has 60 waitlist signups
from validation.

1. **Three weeks out:** write the positioning line, record a 40-second demo (paste link → list
   appears), take four screenshots.
2. **Two weeks out:** pick channels: the waitlist, one cooking subreddit that allows tool
   posts on a weekly thread, and Product Hunt.
3. **One week out:** send the demo to five waitlist people and fix what confuses them.
4. **Launch week:** email the waitlist first, then Product Hunt, then the subreddit thread on
   its allowed day. Reply to everything.
5. **The week after:** email signups who didn't make a list, asking what stopped them. Fix the
   top answer. That fix becomes the next mini-launch.

> 💡 **Try it:** write your product's positioning line using the template above, then send it
> to one person who doesn't know the product and ask them to explain it back in their own
> words. If they can't, rewrite it.

## Common failure modes

- **Launch day is silent** → no audience built beforehand → start the waitlist and share
  progress weeks ahead.
- **Posts removed or ignored** → promotional copy pasted across communities → follow each
  community's rules and lead with the problem.
- **Lots of visits, no signups** → vague positioning or several competing calls to action →
  one clear line, one button.
- **Signups who never come back** → the path to the core moment is too long → shorten
  onboarding and email new users to ask where they stopped.
- **One launch, then nothing** → treating launch as an event → plan the next relaunch before
  this one ends.

## Relaunch (yes, again)

A single launch rarely reaches everyone. Smart builders **relaunch**: for each new channel,
major feature or audience, run the play again. "Launching" is a habit, not a one-time event.
