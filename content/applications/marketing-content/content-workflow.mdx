Great marketing runs on a **content engine**, not random bursts. AI lets you build a
repeatable pipeline that takes an idea all the way to published, and then multiplies it.
The tools matter less than the shape: once you know the stations and where the human sits,
you can run the same line in any assistant and any automation tool.

## The pipeline

1. **Ideate.** AI suggests topics from your niche, the questions your audience asks, and what
   they're reacting to. Your best ideas still come from real conversations; AI turns one into
   ten angles, and can compare what's already published on the topic so you find the gap your
   piece fills.
2. **Outline.** Turn the idea into a structure: the point, three supporting beats, the example
   that proves it. Reviewing an outline takes a minute; fixing a 1,000-word draft takes twenty.
3. **Draft.** AI writes a first pass *in your brand voice* (more on that below).
4. **Edit.** **You** take over: fix accuracy, sharpen voice, add real insight. This is the
   non-negotiable human step.
5. **Format & publish.** Adapt it to the platform: headings for a blog, short lines for
   LinkedIn, a subject line for email.
6. **Repurpose.** The multiplier (below).

## Getting your voice into the draft

"Write in a friendly, professional tone" produces the same voice everyone else gets. What
works is **few-shot prompting**: paste two or three pieces you're proud of and say "match
this voice". Add a short **voice note** next to them:

- three words that describe the voice, each with what it *doesn't* mean ("direct, not rude");
- phrases you never use (many writers ban "game-changer", "unlock" and "in today's
  fast-paced world");
- how you open and close (a question? a story? straight to the point?).

Save the samples and the note in one file or in Project knowledge, so every draft starts from
them instead of from a blank prompt.

## Keep the human at the wheel

AI drafts fast and sounds *fine*, which is the problem. Generic, occasionally wrong content
quietly erodes trust in a brand. So the **editing step is yours**, and it's a checklist, not
a vibe:

- **Facts:** every number, name, price and claim traces to something you know or supplied.
- **One real thing:** a story, an example from your work, an opinion you'd defend. This is
  what makes a piece worth reading.
- **Cut:** delete the throat-clearing intro and the summary conclusion; most AI drafts
  improve when they get shorter.
- **Read it aloud:** anything you'd never say out loud gets rewritten.

## Repurpose: the real leverage

This is where small teams win. One solid piece becomes many: an **article** → a **LinkedIn or
X thread** → **captions** → an **email** → a **short-form video script**.

The rule is *re-angle, don't copy-paste*. A thread leads with the sharpest claim; an email
leads with the reader's situation; a video script needs a hook in the first seconds. Ask for
each format separately, and tell the model which part of the original to lead with.

## Worked example: one question, one evening

A hypothetical freelance bookkeeper keeps hearing "What can I deduct if I work from home?"

1. **Ideate (5 min):** she asks the assistant for five angles on the question; she picks "the
   three things people forget to claim".
2. **Outline (5 min):** point, three items, one real (anonymised) client example she supplies,
   a closing line inviting questions.
3. **Draft (5 min):** the assistant writes 600 words using her two pasted sample posts.
4. **Edit (25 min):** she checks every rule against official guidance for her country, adds
   the client example, cuts the intro and generic advice, and adds a "this isn't tax advice for
   your situation" line.
5. **Repurpose (15 min):** a LinkedIn post leading with the most-forgotten item, a newsletter
   email, and a 30-second video script. She edits each briefly.
6. **Publish:** the article goes on her site; the rest go into the scheduler for the week.

Notice where the time went: most of it was the edit. That's correct.

> 💡 **Try it:** Paste a piece you've already published into your assistant and ask: "Describe
> this writing voice in five bullet points, including what it avoids." Fix anything it gets
> wrong, and you have the first draft of your voice note in under five minutes.

## Automate the glue

Wire the boring parts with **n8n**, Zapier or Make. A simple version uses a spreadsheet or
Notion table with a status column: *Idea → Drafted → Approved → Scheduled*. When a row reaches
*Drafted*, the workflow notifies you; when **you** set it to *Approved*, it sends the post to
your scheduler and logs the date. The approval is the gate, and only a person can open it.
The workflows pillar covers how to build this from scratch (→ first-automation).

## Common failure modes

- **Symptom:** every post sounds like every other AI post. **Cause:** no samples or voice note
  in the prompt. **Fix:** few-shot with your real work and a list of banned phrases.
- **Symptom:** a wrong fact went live. **Cause:** the edit step was skipped under time
  pressure, or the automation had no approval gate. **Fix:** make approval a required status in
  the workflow.
- **Symptom:** your repurposed posts get no engagement. **Cause:** the same text pasted into
  every channel. **Fix:** prompt each format separately with its own lead.
- **Symptom:** the cadence collapses after two weeks. **Cause:** a plan bigger than your real
  hours. **Fix:** time one full pass, then size the weekly plan to that number.
