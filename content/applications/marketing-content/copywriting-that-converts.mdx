Copy is the most valuable writing in marketing: it's the words that make people *act*. AI
can draft it in seconds, but "drafted" and "converts" are different things. The skill is
using AI on top of **proven copy fundamentals** and **real customer insight**, and the second
part is the one most people skip.

## Start from the customer's own words

Strong copy borrows the reader's language. Professional copywriters call this
**voice-of-customer research**, and it's where AI is genuinely useful before a single line of
copy exists. Collect raw material:

- reviews of your product or service, and of competitors (the three-star ones are the most
  honest);
- support emails, sales-call notes, DMs and comments;
- forum and community threads where your audience describes the problem;
- answers to one survey question: "What almost stopped you from buying?"

Paste 20–50 of these into an assistant and ask it to group them into recurring pains, desires
and objections, **quoting the exact phrases** for each. You get a map of what people actually
say, in words that will sound familiar to them on your page. Check the quotes against the
source; models sometimes paraphrase while claiming to quote.

## Feed AI what only you know

AI writes generic copy when you give it generic input. Load it up:

- **Audience:** who, specifically. "Freelance designers who hate chasing invoices," not
  "small businesses".
- **Pains & desires:** in their words, from the research above.
- **Benefits, not features:** for every feature, ask "so what?" until you reach the outcome.
  "Automatic reminders" → "so what?" → "you stop chasing clients" → "you get paid without
  awkward emails". The last answer is the copy.
- **Brand voice:** plus two or three **examples** of copy you love (few-shot prompting).
- **Proof you actually have:** real numbers, real quotes, real results. Nothing else.

## Write within a framework

Don't ask for "some copy". Ask it to write within a structure that has sold things for
decades:

- **PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solution):** strong when the pain is sharp and familiar.
- **AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action):** good for longer pages and emails.
- **BAB (Before → After → Bridge):** paints life now, life after, then your offer as the way
  across. Useful when the outcome is easy to picture.

The framework gives the model a skeleton; your research gives it the flesh.

## Worked example: a service page

A hypothetical freelance web designer wants a hero section for her "website refresh" service.

1. **Research:** she pastes 25 messages from past enquiries. The assistant groups them; the
   top pain, quoted repeatedly, is "embarrassed to send people to our site".
2. **Brief:** audience = owners of small local businesses with dated sites; pain = that
   embarrassment and lost enquiries; benefit = a site they're proud to share, live in four
   weeks; offer = a fixed-price refresh; proof = two real client quotes.
3. **Draft:** she asks for PAS plus a BAB variant. The PAS headline opens on the embarrassment;
   the BAB version opens on "proud to share your link".
4. **Edit:** she removes "cutting-edge" and "seamless", swaps a generic line for a real client
   quote, and checks the four-week promise matches her actual process.
5. **Choose:** she shows both versions to three past clients and asks which one describes their
   situation. The PAS version wins.

> 💡 **Try it:** Take one feature of anything you promote and run the "so what?" ladder out
> loud three times. Write the final answer as a headline. That takes two minutes, and it's
> usually better than the line you had.

## Variants and testing

AI makes variants cheap, so generate many and judge them hard. Ask for variants that differ
in **angle** (pain, outcome, speed, identity), not just wording; five rewordings of one idea
teach you nothing. With plenty of traffic, a proper A/B test settles it. With little traffic,
a split test of small differences can take months to mean anything, so ask a handful of real
readers what each version promises and which they'd click.

## Common failure modes

- **Symptom:** copy full of "unlock", "elevate" and "seamless". **Cause:** no voice samples or
  banned-phrase list. **Fix:** few-shot with copy you like and list words to avoid.
- **Symptom:** invented proof (stats, testimonials, "trusted by thousands"). **Cause:** the
  model fills gaps with plausible claims. **Fix:** a truth rule in the prompt and a manual check
  of every claim.
- **Symptom:** the page lists features and nobody acts. **Cause:** skipped the benefit ladder.
  **Fix:** rewrite each feature as its outcome.
- **Symptom:** "winning" variants that perform the same. **Cause:** testing wording, not angles.
  **Fix:** generate variants that each make a different promise.

## Your unfair advantage

Here's the key mindset (and the video's point): **AI raises the floor, not the ceiling.**
Everyone now has competent, generic copy on tap. Your edge is the human layer: genuine
customer understanding, emotional truth, taste and a voice that sounds real. Use AI to go
faster, not to outsource the thinking. Next: getting found, with SEO.
