Social gets attention; **email converts it.** It's the channel you *own*: no algorithm sits
between you and your list, and if a platform changes its rules tomorrow, your subscribers are
still yours. That's why so many marketers treat it as their best-returning channel. AI makes
building a funnel that turns strangers into customers genuinely fast, but the logic behind it
still has to be right.

## The funnel, simply

A funnel is a path from "just met you" to "bought from you":

1. **Lead magnet:** capture the email with something genuinely useful.
2. **Welcome + nurture sequence:** a series of emails that deliver value and build trust.
3. **Offer:** once you've earned attention, the email that asks for the sale.

## A lead magnet worth an email address

The best lead magnets are **specific, quick to use and connected to what you sell**. "My
newsletter" is weak; "a one-page checklist for preparing your first tax return as a
freelancer" is strong if you sell bookkeeping. It should give a fast, real win, and the
natural next step after that win should be your paid offer. AI can draft a checklist, template
or short guide in minutes; you make sure every item is correct and actually useful.

## The nurture sequence

A common starting shape (adjust to your audience):

- **Email 1, immediately:** deliver the lead magnet and say what's coming.
- **Email 2, a couple of days later:** a quick win they can get today.
- **Email 3:** a story or insight that changes how they see the problem.
- **Email 4:** answer the biggest objection (price, time, "will this work for me?").
- **Email 5:** the offer, with one clear call to action.

Every email should be useful even if the reader never buys. That's what earns the right to
ask. The video's three-email welcome (acknowledge, include, mobilize) is a compact version of
the same arc: start with three, then add the win and objection emails once you see where
readers drop off.

## Triggers, stops and segments

This is where funnels quietly break. Define three things before you write copy:

- **Start trigger:** what puts someone into the sequence (a form signup, a tag, a trial start).
- **Stop rule:** what takes them out (they bought, booked, replied or unsubscribed). Without
  it, buyers get "last chance to buy" emails, which looks careless.
- **Segments:** split by behaviour, not just demographics. Someone who clicked the pricing
  link is in a different place from someone who never opened.

Most email platforms handle these natively. Extra logic, such as tagging a lead when a payment
arrives or notifying you of a hot reply, can be wired with n8n, Zapier or Make.

## Deliverability basics

Great emails in the spam folder convert nobody. The essentials:

- **Authenticate your sending domain** with SPF, DKIM and DMARC records; your platform's setup
  guide walks you through it. Major inbox providers such as Gmail and Yahoo now expect this
  from bulk senders, along with easy one-click unsubscribe.
- **Only email people who opted in.** Never buy lists. Consent rules differ by country, so
  follow your platform's compliance guidance.
- **Remove addresses that never engage**, so your sending reputation stays healthy.

## Where AI helps

- **Drafting sequences and subject-line variants** from your audience brief, then you edit.
- **Personalising** beyond the first name: different examples for different segments.
- **Reading replies:** paste a batch of replies and ask for recurring questions and
  objections, which feed your next emails and your copy.

## Worked example: a coach's funnel

A hypothetical career coach offers a free "CV checklist for career changers".

1. **Trigger:** the signup form adds the tag *cv-checklist* and starts the sequence.
2. **Sequence:** day 0 delivers the checklist; day 2 shows one before-and-after CV line; day 4
   tells the (anonymised) story of a client's switch; day 6 answers "isn't coaching
   expensive?"; day 8 offers a discovery call.
3. **Stop rule:** booking a call (from the scheduling tool, via Zapier) adds the tag *booked*,
   which removes them from the sequence.
4. **Segment:** people who clicked the day 4 story get one extra case study.
5. **Test:** the coach signs up with her own address, reads every email on her phone and books
   a test call to confirm the stop rule fires.

> 💡 **Try it:** Sign up for the lead magnet of someone in your field and save their first
> three emails. Label each one: deliver, win, story, objection or offer. Five minutes, and
> you've reverse-engineered a working sequence.

## Common failure modes

- **Symptom:** buyers keep getting sales emails. **Cause:** no stop rule. **Fix:** an exit on
  the conversion event.
- **Symptom:** emails land in spam. **Cause:** an unauthenticated domain or a stale list.
  **Fix:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC, plus regular list cleaning.
- **Symptom:** high "opens", few clicks. **Cause:** inflated open tracking and a weak call to
  action. **Fix:** judge on clicks and replies; one clear CTA per email.
- **Symptom:** unsubscribes spike after email two. **Cause:** selling before giving value.
  **Fix:** make the early emails genuinely useful.

## The principle

Email rewards **trust over time**, not a single blast. AI lets a solo operator run
personalised funnels that used to need a team, and because you own the list, it compounds.
Last lesson of the track: knowing what's actually working, with analytics.
