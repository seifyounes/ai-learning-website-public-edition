Video is where attention lives, and it used to be where time went to die, because editing is
slow and skilled. AI changes the maths: it removes the editing bottleneck, so you can ship
video without being an editor. What it doesn't change is what makes people watch: a strong
hook, something real to show and a point worth making.

## The three jobs AI handles

1. **Script:** your copywriting and content skills, applied to a hook and a structure. AI
   drafts; you sharpen.
2. **Edit:** AI editors (**Descript, Veed, Submagic, CapCut**) cut silences and filler words,
   add **captions**, suggest **b-roll** and clean up audio. No timeline expertise required.
3. **Generate:** AI can create footage and avatars outright, but be clear-eyed about when
   that's right. For **a product with a screen, it usually isn't**: software sells what it
   shows, so **screen recordings of the real product** delivering its result beat a generated
   presenter. Generation earns its keep in faceless explainer niches and for illustrative
   b-roll. Major platforms also ask creators to label realistic AI-generated content, so check
   each platform's rules before you publish.

## Scripting short-form

Most short videos that work share a simple shape:

- **Hook (first one to three seconds):** name something the viewer recognises, or show the
  result first. No logo, no "hey guys".
- **Payoff:** deliver the one thing the hook promised, as directly as possible.
- **Close:** a single next step (follow, comment, visit) or a line that loops back to the
  start.

Put the key words on screen as text. Plenty of people scroll with the sound off, so captions
and on-screen text carry the message. AI is good at drafting ten hooks for one idea; your job
is picking the one that sounds like a real person talking.

## Editing by transcript

Several editors now let you edit video by **editing its transcript**: delete a sentence in the
text and the matching footage disappears. For talking-head and screen-recording content this
is faster than a timeline and needs no editing skill. Two habits make the output usable:

- **Proofread auto-captions.** Speech recognition stumbles on names, brands and jargon, and a
  misspelled product name in burned-in captions looks careless.
- **Keep a little breathing room.** Aggressive silence removal can make speech sound
  breathless; leave natural pauses where a point needs to land.

## The highest-ROI move: long → short

Short-form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) is where the reach is, but cutting clips by hand takes
hours. AI clipping tools (**Opus Clip** and similar) take **one long video** and output many
captioned short clips, auto-selected for promising moments. Treat their picks as a shortlist,
not a final cut. Film once, post for weeks: the repurposing principle from the content
workflow lesson, supercharged.

## Worked example: one recording, three shorts

A hypothetical online Spanish tutor records a 10-minute talk-to-camera video: "Five mistakes
English speakers make in Spanish".

1. **Transcript:** she uploads it to her editor and exports the timestamped auto-transcript.
2. **Cut list:** she pastes the transcript into her assistant with the task prompt; it suggests
   five moments, ranked.
3. **Review:** she drops two picks that need earlier context and keeps three that stand alone.
4. **Hooks:** each clip gets a new first line on screen, e.g. "You've been saying 'I'm
   embarrassed' wrong", written by her from the AI's suggestions.
5. **Edit:** the editor trims silences and adds captions; she fixes two misheard Spanish words
   in the captions.
6. **Schedule:** three shorts, one every other day, from a single recording session.

> 💡 **Try it:** Open the last video you watched to the end on any short-form platform and
> write down its first line, word for word. Then write three first lines for a clip about your
> own topic in the same pattern. Under five minutes, and you'll start seeing hooks everywhere.

## Common failure modes

- **Symptom:** viewers leave in the first two seconds. **Cause:** a slow intro. **Fix:** open
  on the result or the recognisable problem.
- **Symptom:** clips that make no sense on their own. **Cause:** posting a clipper's picks
  unreviewed. **Fix:** keep only clips that stand alone, and re-hook them.
- **Symptom:** captions with misspelled names and terms. **Cause:** trusting auto-captions.
  **Fix:** proofread every caption track before export.
- **Symptom:** polished videos nobody trusts. **Cause:** stock footage or avatars standing in
  for the real thing. **Fix:** show your real product, work or face.

## Keep your taste

AI gets you most of the way fast, but the **hook, the story and the taste** are still yours.
A snappy edit of a boring message is still boring. Use AI to remove the grunt work so you can
spend your energy on what makes a video worth watching. Next: the visual layer, images and
brand assets.
