Social media rewards **consistency**, and consistency is exactly what burns people out. AI
fixes that: it lets you create a week of posts in an hour and schedule them, so showing up
daily doesn't mean working daily. The part it can't do for you is the part that actually
builds an audience: being a real person in the conversation.

## Pick your lanes first

Every platform has its own formats, culture and pace. Trying to be everywhere means being
mediocre everywhere. Choose **two publishing platforms** and **one community**:

- **Where does your audience already talk about this topic?** Professional and B2B topics
  often live on LinkedIn; visual products on Instagram or Pinterest; fast commentary on X or
  Threads; how-to and entertainment on TikTok and YouTube Shorts.
- **Which format can you sustain?** If you hate being on camera, a video-first platform will
  stall. Pick what you'll still do in month three.
- **One community** (a subreddit, Discord, Slack group or forum) is where you answer questions
  and help, not where you advertise.

## Create (by repurposing)

You already have a content engine from the content workflow lesson. Social is mostly
**repurposing** it: feed AI your core article, video or newsletter and get platform-native
posts. "Native" means more than shorter:

- **The hook changes.** The first line or first second decides whether anyone keeps reading.
- **The format changes.** A carousel breaks one idea into slides; a thread builds an argument
  step by step; a short video needs something to *show*.
- **The tone changes.** LinkedIn tolerates more context; X and Threads reward compression.

It also helps to define three or four **content pillars**: recurring themes you post about
(for example, how-to tips, behind the scenes, customer stories, opinions). They stop you
from starting from zero each week and make your account recognisable. Before you ask for
any of this, **prime the assistant** once: what you offer, who it's for, the lanes you chose,
your pillars and two posts that sound like you. Keep that in a Project or Gem so you never
re-type it.

## Batch and schedule

Batch a week (or a month) of posts in one sitting, then load them into a **scheduler**
(Buffer, Hootsuite and similar tools) to publish automatically. Consistency without the
daily scramble. If you want approved posts to flow straight into the schedule, wire it with
n8n, Zapier or Make (→ first-automation).

## Engage (keep this human)

One rule: **automate creation and scheduling, but keep engagement human.** Replies, DMs and
conversations are where audiences become customers. AI can draft a response; a real person
should send it. Faking engagement is obvious and erodes trust. A practical habit is a short
daily engagement block: reply to everyone who commented, then leave a few genuinely useful
comments on other people's posts in your niche.

## Worked example: a week from one piece

A hypothetical freelance photographer has written a blog post, "What to wear for a family
photo session".

1. **Lanes:** Instagram (visual, where families find photographers) and Facebook (local
   parents' groups), plus one local community group where she answers questions.
2. **Repurpose:** she pastes the post into her assistant and asks for a carousel (five outfit
   rules, one per slide), a short video hook to pair with a behind-the-scenes clip, a story
   post about a real session, and an opinion post ("matching outfits are overrated").
3. **Edit:** she swaps generic advice for her own examples and checks every photo is one she
   has permission to share.
4. **Schedule:** all five go into a scheduler's free plan in twenty minutes.
5. **Engage:** ten minutes a day replying to comments and answering one question in the local
   group, with no link unless someone asks.
6. **Log:** at the end of the week she notes saves, comments and enquiries per post.

> 💡 **Try it:** Pick one post you've already published and ask your assistant: "Rewrite this
> as a LinkedIn post, an X post and a carousel outline, each with a different opening line
> suited to that platform." Compare the three openings. Five minutes, and you'll see what
> "native" means.

## What to watch

Likes are the weakest signal. Watch **saves and shares** (people found it useful), **comments**
(it started a conversation), and **profile visits, link clicks or enquiries** (it moved
someone toward you). Log them per post so the analytics lesson has something to work with.

## Common failure modes

- **Symptom:** low reach on every platform. **Cause:** spreading thin across too many lanes
  with cross-posted text. **Fix:** two lanes, native formats.
- **Symptom:** posts get views but no conversations. **Cause:** no engagement time; you only
  broadcast. **Fix:** a daily reply-and-comment block.
- **Symptom:** your feed reads like a brochure. **Cause:** every post is a pitch. **Fix:**
  content pillars that are mostly useful, with occasional offers.
- **Symptom:** the schedule runs out and nothing replaces it. **Cause:** batching once, never
  again. **Fix:** put a recurring batching session in your calendar.

## The payoff

Create by repurposing, schedule, engage like a human: a consistent social presence in a
fraction of the time. For a freelancer or agency, it's a repeatable, sellable service. Next:
the format eating attention everywhere, video.
