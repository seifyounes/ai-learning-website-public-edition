Using AI assistants for *real* work — coursework, client files, business decisions — means
setting them up properly first. Fifteen minutes in the settings protects you, the people whose
data you handle, and your organization. This lesson is the quick, unglamorous setup that makes
everything before and after it in this pillar safe to use on real work.

## What actually happens to what you type

Three separate things can happen to a conversation, and the settings control them separately:

- **Training** — your chats may be used to improve future models, depending on the product and
  your settings.
- **Retention** — the provider stores your chats for some period, even with training off, for
  history, abuse monitoring or legal reasons. Read the retention terms; they differ by product and
  tier.
- **Memory** — the assistant saves facts about you and feeds them into future chats.

Business, enterprise and education tiers usually come with contractual terms (no training on your
data by default, set retention, admin controls), which is why your employer or university may
require them. Check your organization's terms rather than assuming.

## Turn off model training

The most important toggle: **data controls / "improve the model for everyone."** Turn it **off**
before you put any work or client data in. Where it lives (names drift; search the settings or
help center if they've moved):

- **ChatGPT:** Settings → **Data Controls** → "Improve the model for everyone" → off.
- **Claude:** Settings → **Privacy** → review the "Help improve Claude" option and set it
  deliberately.
- **Gemini:** the activity setting at myactivity.google.com (labelled **Gemini Apps Activity**,
  renamed **Keep Activity** in newer versions) — turning it off limits what's kept and used.

All three also offer a **temporary or incognito chat** that isn't saved to your history or used
for memory, though providers may keep it briefly for safety review. Use it for one-off sensitive
questions. Set all three apps, not just the one you use most.

## Know the never-paste line

Opting out of training reduces one risk, but it doesn't make everything safe. **Personal data
about other people, contracts, unreleased financials, credentials and secrets don't belong in a
consumer-tier chatbot** — opt-out or not. For genuinely sensitive material, use an **approved
enterprise tier** (with a contractual data guarantee) or a local model. Match the tool to the
sensitivity of the data. The organizational version of this rule is covered in the AI-in-companies
track (→ governance-security).

> 💡 **Try it:** open your main assistant's memory or personalization page right now and read what
> it has saved about you. Delete one thing you wouldn't want a colleague to see. Two minutes.

## Scrub instead of skipping

The never-paste line doesn't mean "don't get help." It means **send the structure, not the
secrets**. Replace names with roles, swap real numbers for realistic fake ones, cut account
numbers and contact details. For most tasks — fixing a formula, tightening an email, finding a
bug, restructuring a report — the assistant needs the shape of the problem, not the real people.

**Worked example.** You want help replying to an angry customer email. Instead of pasting the
thread, paste: "Customer A ordered product X on [date], received it damaged, and our first reply
offered a 10% discount, which they called insulting. Our policy allows a full refund or
replacement. Draft a calm reply offering both." You get the same quality of draft, and the
customer's name, address and order number never left your inbox.

## Manage memory, connectors and shared links

Memory is convenient, but it's *storing information about you*. Periodically **review what it's
saved**, clear anything sensitive, and **turn it off for confidential projects** so details don't
leak into unrelated chats.

Two newer surfaces deserve the same care:

- **Connectors** (Drive, email, calendar) let an assistant read your files. Connect only what a
  task needs, and disconnect what you no longer use.
- **Shared chat links** can be opened by anyone who has the link. Check what's in a chat before you
  share it, and delete old shared links you no longer need.

## Secure the account

Your AI account now holds a surprising amount: chat history, uploaded files, memory, custom
assistants — a detailed picture of your work. Treat it like the sensitive login it is: **strong,
unique password + 2FA**, on the AI account and on any Google, Microsoft or GitHub account it signs
in through. Delete old chats you won't reopen: a chat history is as sensitive as an email
archive if someone gets into your device or account. And consider **separate accounts** for personal vs work or client use so data, memory
and custom assistants don't bleed across contexts.

## Common failure modes

- **You assume opt-out means private** → retention and policy still apply → use approved tools
  for sensitive data and scrub everything else.
- **An old client's details show up in a new client's draft** → memory carried them across →
  separate accounts or Projects, and memory off for confidential work.
- **A shared chat link exposes more than you meant** → the whole conversation is visible → review
  before sharing, and delete old links.
- **You configured one app and use three** → defaults differ per tool → run the checklist on every
  assistant you actually open.

## The professional baseline

Run the checklist once per tool: training off, memory reviewed, connectors scoped, 2FA on,
retention terms read, and a clear personal "never paste this" rule. Do it and you can use these
assistants on real work with confidence instead of crossing your fingers.
