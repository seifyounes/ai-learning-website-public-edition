Lesson 8 gave you retrieval and then named its ceiling: every query re-derives the answer
from raw chunks, so nothing accumulates. This lesson is the alternative, and it's the one
the whole pillar has been walking toward.

The shift is small to say and large to run. **Stop retrieving from your sources. Compile
them.** Have an agent read each source once, distil it into pages, cross-link it against
everything already there — and then keep those pages current as new material arrives. Ask
a question and it reads pages that already hold the synthesis.

## The source: an idea file, not a tool

The pattern comes from a public gist by **Andrej Karpathy** — `llm-wiki.md`, April 2026.
It is deliberately abstract. You don't install it; you paste it into your agent (or a chat
assistant, along with your vault's rules) and work with it to instantiate a version that fits
your material. That's the intended use, stated in the document itself.

Two things the blogs around it get wrong:

- **It is agent-agnostic.** The gist names Codex, Claude Code and OpenCode as equally valid
  hosts, and its example schema files are `AGENTS.md` for Codex or CLAUDE.md for Claude Code.
  It is not a one-vendor technique.
- **Obsidian's role is human review.** He runs the agent on one side and Obsidian on the
  other, browsing the results as they're written. His one-line summary: *"Obsidian is the
  IDE; the LLM is the programmer; the wiki is the codebase."*

Which is exactly this pillar's framing: **Obsidian is the human side, and the agent owns the
files.**

## Three layers

You already have the folders from lesson 2. Now they get roles:

- **Raw sources — `sources/`.** Articles, papers, transcripts, exports. You curate this; it
  is **immutable**. The agent reads it and never writes to it, so you can always check the
  wiki against it.
- **The wiki — `wiki/`.** Summaries, entity pages, concept pages, comparisons. The agent owns
  this layer *entirely*. You read it, follow its links, and catch what's wrong.
- **The schema — `vault-rules.md`.** How pages are named, what the conventions are, what
  workflow to follow when ingesting, answering or linting. This turns a general-purpose agent
  into a disciplined wiki maintainer. Expect to rewrite it three times in the first month.

The lesson-6 concept pages you already wrote are the seed of layer 2. From here, the agent
grows it.

## Three operations

**Ingest.** You drop a source in and tell the agent to process it. It reads the source,
talks the takeaways through with you, writes a summary page, updates the index, revises
every entity and concept page the source touches, and appends to the log. One source
routinely touches **ten to fifteen pages** — the work you would never do by hand.

**Query.** You ask the wiki, not the sources. The agent reads the relevant pages and answers
with citations. The move that makes this compound: **good answers get filed back into the
wiki as new pages.** In a chat-only workflow they evaporate; here they become part of the
base.

**Lint.** A health check: contradictions between pages, claims a newer source has
superseded, orphan pages, concepts mentioned everywhere but lacking a page, missing
cross-references. Tonight's run is scoped to `wiki/` so you've seen the operation once;
lesson 10 turns it into a weekly habit over the whole vault.

## The two spine files

**`index.md`** is a catalog of every page with a link and a one-line summary, grouped by
category. The agent updates it on every ingest and reads it *first* when answering. At your
scale this is the whole retrieval system: a table of contents plus a good reader beats an
embedding index, with no infrastructure at all.

**`log.md`** is chronological and append-only — ingests, queries, lint passes, each dated.
Give every entry the same prefix and one Obsidian search for `ingest |` shows the last things
that happened to your vault. It's how the AI knows what it already did.

## Don't code? The same loop with a chat assistant

The pattern needs the three layers and someone to do the file moves. With a chat assistant,
that someone is you. `vault-rules.md` goes into a Project's or Gem's instructions, where it
loads on every chat. For each ingest, upload `index.md` and the pages it lists, ask for new
pages as separate markdown blocks and edits as marked lines, then save them and add the log
line yourself. It's slower, and the uploads go stale unless you refresh them after each
ingest; the layers, rules and review job are identical.

## The judgment half — and this video's errata

Milo's contribution is the part the gist skips: what stops this from quietly replacing your
own thinking. His **IDI** loop — *imagine, discern, integrate* — puts a mandatory human step
between generation and belief, and at [06:06] he demonstrates the middle one honestly,
rejecting a conclusion about his own last 45 days on camera. He also writes every AI output
into a **separate vault**, so nothing generated slips into his own words without a decision.

Corrections before you take anything from the video as current:

- **[09:58] — Bases called "new."** Bases has been a **core** plugin since Obsidian **1.9

- **He's on macOS.** Nothing in the workflow is platform-specific, so ignore the shortcuts.

And two things that are selling, not teaching: a **paid-workshop QR code at [09:47–10:09]**
and an **archetype-quiz funnel at [12:50–13:26]**. Stop at **[10:11]**; the prompts in the

## Before you run it

This loop edits more files than anything else in this pillar. Take Milo's advice literally:
**back up your vault first.** Then hold two rules, both worth putting in `vault-rules.md` (and
your Project's instructions) — never overwrite without asking:

- **Plan first, or write alongside.** Any prompt that touches existing pages must show a plan
  or a diff and wait. Any prompt that generates must write *new* files.
- **Lint reports; you approve.** A lint pass proposes fixes. It never applies them.

> 💡 **Try it:** before any ingest, ask your agent (or your Vault Project, with `index.md`
> uploaded) one read-only question: "Read `index.md` and list the three wiki pages you would
> update if I ingested an article about [a topic you study or work on]. Change nothing." If
> its answer is sensible, your schema and index are doing their job; if not, fix
> `vault-rules.md` before the first real ingest.

When you've done ingest, query and lint on real sources, you own the loop — and lesson 10 is
about keeping it alive.
