A vault that only grows is a vault that rots. Every week you add sources, notes and concept
pages, and every week a few of them quietly go wrong: a claim superseded by something you read
on Thursday, two pages asserting opposite things, a note nothing links to.

That is what the weekly review is really for, and it has a better name: **Lint**, the third
operation of the loop you built in lesson 9. Ingest fills the vault, query uses it, and Lint
keeps it *true*. It's also the one nobody does, because it's pure bookkeeping — precisely the
work an AI doesn't get bored doing.

## Four moves, and AI runs the first one

1. **Lint** *(a coding agent or a chat assistant)* — sweep the vault and report what's broken.
   It produces a dated report and changes nothing else.
2. **Process** *(you)* — drain `inbox/` and the week's `daily/` notes. Link it, distill it, or
   delete it. Inbox to zero.
3. **Update PARA** *(you)* — move notes between buckets as their status changed. Shipped work
   goes Projects → Archive; a new commitment gets promoted.
4. **Plan** *(you)* — decide next week's 1–3 real priorities.

**Don't code?** Move 1 still works two ways: upload the vault's files to a chat assistant with
the task's chat prompt, or run a lighter lint with Obsidian alone (graph view's Orphans filter,
and the lesson-7 base for pages missing a property).

Lint doesn't replace the three human moves; it does their homework. Processing an inbox is fast
when something already listed the unlinked notes, and planning is fast when the gaps are named.
Without the sweep you spend the review on archaeology and run out of patience before deciding.

## What Lint actually looks for

- **Orphans** — pages nothing links to. Graph view shows them as floating dots.
- **Stale claims** — a page states something a later source contradicts. The most valuable
  class and the hardest to catch by hand, because the note still *reads* fine.
- **Contradictions** — two pages that can't both be right. Usually a decision you revisited
  and only recorded in one place.
- **Missing cross-references** — page A discusses B at length and B never mentions A.
- **Gaps** — `index.md` promises a page that doesn't exist, or a source got ingested and no
  concept page came out of it.

The base you built in lesson 7 is a good companion: it shows the property-level version of the
same question — untagged pages, missing dates, sources with no status.

> 💡 **Try it:** open graph view, turn on "Orphans" in its filters if it's off, and count the
> unconnected dots. Click two of them. For each, decide in ten seconds: link it, merge it, or
> let it go. That's the Lint judgment in miniature.

## It reports; you decide

The rule that makes Lint safe on real notes: **it proposes, it never rewrites.** The findings
are genuinely ambiguous. A contradiction usually means you changed your mind, and only you know
which side won. An orphan is sometimes junk and sometimes a seed you haven't written toward. A
"stale" claim is occasionally the one that was right all along.

So Lint produces two artifacts: a dated report you walk top to bottom, and one line in
`log.md`. You mark each finding keep, fix or drop; only then does a second, scoped pass apply
what you approved, whether an agent makes the edits or you do. Give an AI write access to your
own conclusions and you don't get a maintained vault; you get one you no longer trust.

## Plus, minus, next — the reflection half

The video's first four minutes give you the other half of a review: looking back on the week
as a person rather than as a file system. Anne-Laure Le Cunff's format is three headings and
about five minutes: **+** for what worked, **−** for what didn't, **→** for what you'll do
next. It covers every area of your life and keeps you from relitigating Tuesday.

It's a *reflection* structure, so it feeds move 4, Plan, and has nothing to say about your inbox
or your PARA buckets. That's the honest limit of the video: it solves one third of the review,
and solves it well.

## The template

Friction kills reviews, so Friday should start from a fill-in. Save this in your templates
folder as `Weekly review.md`:

```markdown
# Weekly review — <% tp.date.now("YYYY-[W]ww") %>

## 0 · Lint report
- [ ] `lint-<date>.md` read; every finding marked keep / fix / drop

## 1 · Process inbox & daily notes
- [ ] `inbox/` at zero; keepers linked or distilled

## 2 · Update PARA
- Moves made (Projects → Archive, etc.):

## 3 · Plus / minus / next
### +
### −
### →

## 4 · Next week's top 3
1.
2.
3.
```

Monthly and quarterly reviews are a fine addition once this one is a habit. Weekly is the one
that matters.

The rhythm from here is small: **ingest during the week, query when you need an answer, lint on
Friday.** You curate and decide; the AI does the bookkeeping. That's a knowledge base that
compounds instead of rotting, in plain markdown, on your own disk.
